How to Write a Video Script for Instagram Reels

Writing for Reels is completely different from writing a caption or blog post. A good Reel script is built for the ear—short sentences, bold emphasis words, and a clean three-part arc.

Reading time: 6 min Category: Video Scripts Updated: November 26, 2026

Target Word Counts by Reel Length

The Hook–Body–Close Script Framework

Part 1: The Hook (First 3 Seconds / 10–18 Words)

State the problem, result, or surprising contrast immediately. Avoid warm-up throat clearing. Strong hook patterns include:

  • Mistake callout: "Stop doing [common habit] if you want [desired outcome]."
  • Specific result: "How to [achieve outcome] in [short time] without [annoying obstacle]."
  • Direct question: "Why do your [videos/projects] keep stalling right after [moment]?"

Part 2: The Body (Value in Short Chunks)

Deliver one clear idea—or at most three numbered steps—using short sentences under 14 words each. Use line breaks and **bold** keywords when reading on a teleprompter so your eye catches emphasis cues effortlessly.

Part 3: The Close (Clear Next Step)

End cleanly on a specific takeaway or action rather than trailing off with "Anyway, yeah, hope that helps."

Complete 95-Word Reels Script Example

[HOOK] Stop deleting your Reels just because your first take felt stiff.

[BODY] Most creators think confidence means speaking without any script. Instead, write three short lines—and read them right next to your phone lens.
First: cut sentences to ten words or fewer.
Second: take one slow breath before tapping record.
Third: cap yourself at three takes so perfectionism doesn't win.

[CLOSE] Save this script structure before you film tomorrow's video.
